Figure sources

Every figure in the Generative AI chapters is an original plot generated for these notes on 24 September 2026 with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, PyTorch, and scikit-learn; no external artwork or course slides are reproduced. Each chapter’s figures come from one script in Sources/Figure code, which is the complete record of the construction. The shared module genfig.py sets the palette, fonts, and export (white background, 200 dpi PNG). The scripts of chapters 1–3, 7, and 10–12 use scikit-learn’s bundled \(8\times8\) digits (1,797 images with intensities 0–16) with the module’s split: a permutation by NumPy default_rng(0), the first 1,500 images for training and the other 297 held out. Chapters 2, 3, and 12 binarize the digits at intensity \(>7\), and chapters 7, 10, and 11 scale them to \([-1,1]\) by \(x/8-1\). The other figures use synthetic distributions generated in the scripts: two moons, mixtures of Gaussians on a line and on a circle, points on a circle, and demonstrations of a steering task. No script loads a file or depends on another, and every model is trained from scratch when its script runs. Data sources describes the data. The tables below summarize the data, seeds, and model settings of each figure so that it can be read and regenerated without opening the script; the computing setup gives the environment and commands.

Seeds refer to torch.manual_seed (“torch seed”), torch.Generator().manual_seed (“generator seed”), or numpy.random.default_rng (“NumPy seed”); scikit-learn’s random_state is named where it is used, and layers whose initialization is not described keep PyTorch’s default. Draws without their own generator, such as minibatches, training noise, and fresh samples of synthetic data, continue the stream of the script’s torch or NumPy seed in the order of the script. The receptive fields of chapter 2 and the Dirac GAN of chapter 5 involve no randomness. No script reads command-line arguments; each always writes all its figures. The scripts that train with PyTorch set torch.set_num_threads(1), except ch02_autoregressive.py, ch03_vae.py, and ch04_flows.py, which use PyTorch’s default; ch05_gans.py and ch08_sde.py set it before their second figure, the first to use PyTorch. According to their docstrings, ch01_foundations.py and ch13_eval.py take seconds, ch04_flows.py, ch05_gans.py, ch14_policy.py, and ch15_memorization.py about half a minute each, ch02_autoregressive.py, ch09_flow.py, ch11_latent.py, and ch12_masked.py about a minute, ch06_score.py and ch08_sde.py about two minutes, ch03_vae.py about three, ch07_diffusion.py about four, and ch10_guidance.py about six. Training results are single runs; they illustrate the claims in the notes and are not benchmarks.

3. Variational autoencoders {#3-variational-autoencoders}

Script: ch03_vae.py. Both figures use VAEs of the binarized digits with the module’s split (NumPy seed 0): a diagonal Gaussian encoder with two hidden layers of 256 ReLU units, a decoder of the same shape with Bernoulli logits, and a standard normal prior. Each model is trained for 300 epochs of shuffled minibatches of 100 with Adam at learning rate \(10^{-3}\) on distortion plus \(\beta\) times rate, with one reparameterized sample per digit; the models are trained in sequence after one torch seed 0. Rate is the closed-form KL divergence from the prior and distortion the binary cross-entropy of one sample, both reported in bits per image.

10. Guidance and conditional generation {#10-guidance-and-conditional-generation}

Script: ch10_guidance.py. Both figures use one class-conditional DDPM of the digits scaled to \([-1,1]\) with the module’s split (NumPy seed 0), with the linear schedule of chapter 7 (\(T=1000\), \(\beta_t\) from \(10^{-4}\) to 0.02). 15. The theory of diffusion models {#15-the-theory-of-diffusion-models}

Script: ch15_memorization.py.

Local figureData and construction
gen-theory-memorization.pngTraining sets of 32, 8, and 512 points at uniform angles on a circle of radius 2, drawn in that order from one generator (seed 0). The denoiser has the preconditioning of chapter 8 with \(\sigma_{\text{data}}=\sqrt2\) and an MLP with three hidden layers of 128 SiLU units; each network is trained from torch seed 0 for 4,000 steps of 256 training points drawn with replacement, with \(\ln\sigma\sim\mathcal N(-1.2,1.2^2)\) and Adam at learning rate \(10^{-3}\). The exact denoiser of the 32 points is the average of the training points \(x_i\) weighted by the softmax of \(-\lVert x-x_i\rVert^2/(2\sigma^2)\). Sampling: 2,000 points from \(80\,z\) (generator seed 1, the same in each panel) by Heun’s method on 40 levels from 80 to 0.002 with \(\rho=7\), with a final Euler step to 0. Panels: the exact denoiser of 32 points, and networks trained on 8, 32, and 512 points, with the circle in gray and the training points circled in orange. The titles give the share of samples within a tenth of the mean gap \(4\pi/N\) of a training point and the share of 100 equal arcs of the circle that contain a sample.

Shared style

genfig.py selects Matplotlib’s Agg backend, and its setup() sets the font DejaVu Sans (10.5 pt, titles 11.5 pt, DejaVu Sans math text), dark axes of line width 0.8 without top and right spines, dark ticks, frameless legends, and white figure, axes, and saved backgrounds. Its save() writes each figure to Sources/Images as a 200 dpi PNG with a tight bounding box and 0.08 inches of padding. The palette is BLUE #2267a5, ORANGE #d46a23, GREEN #39805a, PURPLE #995aa4, RED #b32b35, DARK #152330 for axes and ticks, GRAY #586473, and LIGHT #c1c8cf, with light tints of the first five colors defined for fills; the default color cycle is blue, orange, green, purple, red, and gray. Individual scripts set smaller font sizes for titles, annotations, and legends, and use Matplotlib’s gray_r, gray, Blues, RdBu, and tab10 colormaps for images and classes.
