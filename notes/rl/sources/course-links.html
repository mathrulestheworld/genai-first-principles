DeepMind x UCL Reinforcement Learning Lecture Series, 2021 — Hado van Hasselt, Diana Borsa, and Matteo HesselLecture playlist · Unofficial slide mirrorMain course: thirteen lectures on the foundations, from bandits, Markov decision processes, and the theory of dynamic programming to model-free prediction and control, function approximation, planning and models, policy gradients, multi-step and off-policy learning, and deep RL (chapters 1–3, 5–13, 16, and 23). YouTube labels the playlist as a deep-learning series, but it holds the RL lectures. No official slides remain online; the mirror collects the thirteen decks.
UCL, 2015 — David Silver, Introduction to Reinforcement LearningCourse page with slides · Lecture playlist · Easy21 assignment · PDFMain course: the classic ten-lecture course, with slides for every lecture: MDPs, dynamic programming, model-free prediction and control, value function approximation, policy gradients, learning and planning, exploration, and games (chapters 1–3, 5–8, 10–11, 13, 24, and 27). Its Easy21 assignment applies Monte Carlo control, SARSA(λ), and linear function approximation to a card game.
UC Berkeley CS285, Fall 2023 and Spring 2026 — Sergey Levine, Deep Reinforcement LearningCourse page with slides · Lecture playlist · Spring 2026 course · Spring 2026 playlistMain course: the standard deep RL course: imitation, policy gradients, actor-critic, Q-learning, optimal control, model-based RL, exploration, offline RL, inverse RL, control as inference, and RL theory (chapters 1, 11, 13, 15–16, 19–23, 25–26, and 29–31). Each Fall 2023 lecture is split into several videos. The Spring 2026 edition supplies the lectures on Q-learning in practice and on RL with sequences and language models (chapters 18 and 28). Its homeworks implement imitation learning, policy gradients, Q-learning and actor-critic methods, model-based RL, and exploration and offline RL.
Stanford CS234, Spring 2024 — Emma Brunskill, Reinforcement LearningCourse page with slides · Lecture playlistSupplement: sixteen lectures, strongest on data-efficient RL: tabular planning, model-free evaluation and control, policy search, bandits and regret, Bayesian and PAC exploration, offline RL and policy evaluation, imitation and learning from preferences, and Monte Carlo tree search (chapters 1–5, 7, 9–10, 13, 20, 22, and 24–26).
Stanford CS224R, Spring 2025 and 2026 — Chelsea Finn, Deep Reinforcement LearningCourse page with slides · Spring 2025 playlistSupplement: deep RL for robotics and language models: imitation, off-policy actor-critic, offline RL, reward learning, RL for language-model reasoning, multi-task and goal-conditioned RL, meta-RL, hierarchy, sim-to-real, and RL fine-tuning of vision–language–action models (chapters 21, 25–26, 28–29, and 31). The reading plan links the 2026 slides; the 2025 recordings cover the same lectures under the same numbers.
Pieter Abbeel, Foundations of Deep RL, 2021Lecture playlistSupplement: six compact lectures: exact methods and maximum-entropy RL, deep Q-learning, policy gradients and advantage estimation, TRPO and PPO, DDPG and SAC, and model-based RL (chapters 13, 16, 20–21, and 23).
University of Alberta — Martha White and Adam White, Reinforcement Learning SpecializationCourseraSupplement for Part I: four short courses that follow Sutton and Barto closely: bandits, MDPs, and dynamic programming; Monte Carlo, TD, Q-learning, and Dyna; prediction and control with function approximation and policy gradients; and a capstone (chapters 1–3, 5–7, 10–11, and 13).
UC Berkeley CS287, Fall 2019 — Pieter Abbeel, Advanced RoboticsCourse page with slides and videosSupplement for control and partial observability: exact methods and function approximation for MDPs, LQR and iterative LQR, trajectory optimization and model-predictive control, Bayes filters, POMDPs, imitation, and sim-to-real transfer (chapters 11, 14–15, 25, and 31).
CMU 16-745, 2025 — Zac Manchester, Optimal Control and Reinforcement LearningLectures and notes · Lecture playlist · NotebooksSupplement for control: LQR from three viewpoints, convex MPC, differential dynamic programming, and direct trajectory optimization, with lecture notebooks (chapter 15).
Harvard CS2824, Spring 2026 — Kianté Brantley and Sham Kakade, Foundations of Reinforcement LearningCourse page with slidesSupplement for theory: sample complexity, exploration, function approximation, policy optimization, and offline RL with proofs, taught by two of the authors of RLTA (chapter 30).
Cornell CS6789, Fall 2024 — Wen SunCourse page with slidesSupplement for theory: slides on value iteration, policy iteration and linear programming, multi-armed, contextual, and linear bandits, fitted Q-iteration, sample complexity, UCBVI, and Bellman rank (chapters 2–4, 12, and 30).
University of Alberta CMPUT 653 — Csaba SzepesváriLecture notesSupplement for theory: lecture notes on the theory of planning and learning in MDPs, with function approximation (chapter 30).
Princeton ECE524, Spring 2024 — Chi Jin, Foundations of Reinforcement LearningLecture playlistSupplement for theory: recorded lectures on the foundations of RL theory (chapter 30).
Stefano Albrecht, Filippos Christianos, and Lukas Schäfer, Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning lecturesLecture playlist · SlidesSupplement for multi-agent RL: lectures that follow the authors’ book on games, solution concepts, and multi-agent deep RL (chapter 27).
Nathan Lambert, Reinforcement Learning from Human FeedbackBook · CourseSupplement for language models: the book and lectures on post-training, with policy-gradient algorithms for language models and reasoning; lectures 3–5 and chapter 6 of the book serve chapter 28.
